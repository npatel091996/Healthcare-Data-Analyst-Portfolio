Clinical Trial Data Analysis – SAS

Restricted data

Health Data Analytics with SAS (BINF5210) · Fall 2023

Analyzed a primarily synthetic clinical trial dataset (random sample of n = 1,000) using ANOVA and found no statistically significant drug effect on glucose test scores, F(2, 997) = 2.17, p = 0.1142, through SAS exploratory data analysis and hypothesis testing.

  • 2,602

    patients after cleaning and merge

  • n = 1,000

    simple random sample for ANOVA

  • p = 0.1142

    no significant drug effect on glucose

  • r = 0.618

    length of stay vs. total charges (p < .0001)

Problem
Does Drug A lower fasting blood glucose? Participants were randomly assigned to Placebo, Low Dose, or High Dose Drug A, and glucose readings were taken after ten weeks.
Methodology
Cleaned two patient-level datasets in SAS by removing records with missing values and invalid "n/a" group labels, deduplicating on Patient ID, and inner-joining them into 2,602 patients. Profiled the data with descriptive statistics, box plots, and pie charts, then drew a random sample of 1,000 (PROC SURVEYSELECT). Checked ANOVA assumptions with PROC UNIVARIATE and Levene's test (p = 0.3046), and ran a one-way ANOVA in PROC GLM because group sizes were unbalanced.
Results / Impact
Mean glucose scores did not differ significantly across Placebo (102.41), Low Dose (90.82), and High Dose (98.89) groups. Length of stay correlated with total charges (r = 0.618). Because scores were not normally distributed, a Kruskal-Wallis test is recommended to confirm the ANOVA result.
